Theory Questions and Answers

Question

Define signal, symbol, information content, entropy, conditional entropy, and joint entropy.

A signal is a measurable physical quantity over time. A symbol is a defined signal state or change that represents information. Information content is I(x)=-_2 p(x). Entropy is average information H(X)=_x p(x)I(x). Conditional entropy measures remaining uncertainty when another variable is known. Joint entropy measures uncertainty of a pair of random variables.

Question

Why does a rare symbol carry more information?

A rare symbol is less predictable, so observing it resolves more uncertainty. Since -_2 p(x) grows when p(x) becomes smaller, rare symbols have higher information content.

Question

When is entropy maximal?

Entropy is maximal when all symbols are equally likely. A binary source has maximum entropy 1 bit at p(0)=p(1)=0.5. An alphabet of M equally likely symbols has entropy _2 M bits per symbol.

Question

Distinguish time/value continuity.

  • Time-continuous: defined for every time instant.
  • Time-discrete: defined only at sample instants.
  • Value-continuous: arbitrary amplitudes are possible.
  • Value-discrete: only fixed amplitude levels are possible.
  • Sampled but unquantized: time-discrete and value-continuous.
  • Digital bit stream: time-discrete and value-discrete.
Question

Fourier series or Fourier transform?

Fourier series represents periodic signals as sums of harmonics. Fourier transform represents general aperiodic signals in the frequency domain. Use Fourier series when the signal is periodic or constructed by periodic repetition of a ground pulse.

Question

State the sampling theorem.

A signal bandlimited to B can be reconstructed from samples if f_A 2B. Below that, aliasing occurs: high-frequency components appear as wrong lower-frequency components and information is lost.

Question

Sampling versus quantization?

Sampling discretizes time. Quantization discretizes amplitude. For linear quantization over interval [a,b] with M levels, the step width is approximately =(b-a)/(M-1) for levels including both endpoints; with rounding, the maximum error inside the interval is about /2.

Question

Explain SNR and dB conversion.

Linear SNR=P_S/P_N. In dB, SNR_dB=10_10(SNR). Convert back by SNR=10^SNR_dB/10.

Question

Compare Hartley and Shannon-Hartley capacity.

Hartley depends on bandwidth and the number of distinguishable signal levels. Shannon-Hartley depends on bandwidth and noise: C=B_2(1+SNR). Hartley focuses on symbol distinguishability; Shannon gives a noise-limited upper bound.

Question

Source coding, channel coding, line coding?

  • Source coding: removes redundancy, e.g. Huffman compression.
  • Channel coding: adds structured redundancy for error detection/correction.
  • Line coding: maps bits or code words to physical signal shapes suitable for the medium.
Question

Manchester, NRZ, MLT-3, ASK, PSK, and QAM?

  • NRZ: line code.
  • Manchester: line code with a transition in every bit interval.
  • MLT-3: multi-level line code.
  • ASK: changes amplitude.
  • PSK: changes phase.
  • QAM: uses amplitude in two quadrature components and is often visualized as a constellation.
Question

For M-QAM, how many bits are transmitted per symbol?

  • General rule: log2(M) bits per symbol.
  • 16-QAM: 4 bits/symbol.
  • 32-QAM: 5 bits/symbol.
  • 1024-QAM: 10 bits/symbol.
Question

Why does higher-order modulation need better SNR?

More constellation points are closer together, so noise can more easily move a received point across a decision boundary. Better SNR is needed to distinguish symbols reliably.

Question

Compare electrical, optical, and wireless media.

  • Electrical copper: cheap and common, but affected by attenuation and electromagnetic interference.
  • Optical fiber: high rates, long distances, immune to electromagnetic interference.
  • Wireless: flexible and mobile, but shared, noisy, and collision/interference-prone.
Question

Propagation delay versus serialization delay?

Propagation delay is travel time through the medium: t_p=d/v. Serialization delay is time to put all bits on the link: t_s=L/R. Long distances increase propagation delay; large frames or low bitrates increase serialization delay.

Old Exam Questions and Answers

Midterm 2020, Aufgabe 2

Question: Explain Shannon versus Hartley capacity and quantization.

Answer: Shannon capacity is noise-limited using B and SNR; Hartley capacity uses bandwidth and distinguishable symbols. Quantization maps continuous amplitudes to discrete levels and introduces bounded error inside the quantization interval.

Midterm 2020, Aufgabe 4

Question: Encode a message and protect packets with CRC.

Answer: Choose the source representation, e.g. BCD for digits or ASCII otherwise, split into packets, append CRC checksum, and validate by polynomial division at the receiver.

Midterm 2021, Aufgabe 2

Question: Use 4-ASK with Manchester coding in a frame format.

Answer: 4-ASK carries 2 raw bits per modulation symbol; Manchester introduces transitions for clock recovery and typically doubles the required signaling effort for the same payload bits.

Midterm 2021, Aufgabe 3

Question: Determine signal levels from a 16-bit converter.

Answer: A 16-bit value can represent 2^16=65536 different levels.

Midterm 2022, Aufgabe 1

Question: Match signals to spectra and reason about Fourier series.

Answer: Periodic time-domain signals have discrete line spectra; pulse shape determines spectral envelope.

Midterm 2022, Aufgabe 4

Question: Compute end-to-end delay in a transmission chain.

Answer: Add serialization, propagation, processing, and waiting times; for pipelined segments, account for overlap instead of multiplying blindly.

Midterm 2023, Aufgabe 1

Question: Identify time-discrete/value-continuous signals and highest-information symbols.

Answer: Time-discrete signals exist only at sample instants; value-continuous signals can take arbitrary amplitudes. Highest information belongs to the least frequent symbol.

Midterm 2023, Aufgabe 2

Question: Decide whether Fourier series applies.

Answer: It applies when the given signal is periodic; otherwise use the Fourier transform.

Endterm 2023, Aufgabe 5

Question: Analyze a periodically repeated ground pulse.

Answer: Draw one period, repeat it with period T, then use Fourier-series coefficients for periodic frequency analysis.

Endterm 2023, Aufgabe 6

Question: Explain channel codes and line codes.

Answer: Channel codes add redundancy to detect/correct errors; line codes map bits/codewords to physical signal waveforms.

Endterm 2024, Aufgabe 1

Question: Answer MC on sampling, quantization, QAM, serialization, propagation, and CRC.

Answer: Sampling discretizes time, quantization discretizes values, 1024-QAM carries 10 bits/symbol, serialization is L/R, propagation is d/v, and CRC detects but does not repair errors.

Endterm 2024, Aufgabe 2

Question: Complete a transmission chain with Huffman, coding, and CRC.

Answer: Source coding compresses, channel/CRC redundancy protects against errors, line/modulation maps bits to signals.

Endterm 2025, Aufgabe 1

Question: Compute sampling interval for f_A=20 kHz and entropy.

Answer: T_A=1/20000=50 µ s; entropy is - p_i_2 p_i.

Endterm 2025, Aufgabe 6

Question: Infer modulation and bitrate from a constellation and basisband signal.

Answer: Count constellation points for bits/symbol, combine with symbol rate and coding rate for effective data rate.

Retake 2022, Aufgabe 3

Question: Recognize and use Manchester coding.

Answer: Manchester represents bits by transitions inside a bit interval, supporting clock recovery at the cost of more bandwidth.

Retake 2022, Aufgabe 4

Question: Determine information content and delay/rate tradeoffs.

Answer: Use I(x)=-_2 p(x), and compute delay from serialization plus propagation over each hop.

Retake 2024, Aufgabe 1

Question: Select known digital modulation methods and highest-information symbol.

Answer: ASK, PSK, QAM are modulation methods; the rarest symbol has highest information content.

Retake 2024, Aufgabe 2

Question: Analyze periodic EKG-like signal.

Answer: Draw the periodic waveform and compute Fourier-series terms using the period and ground pulse.

Retake 2024, Aufgabe 4

Question: Compute satellite transmission performance.

Answer: Propagation dominates for astronomical distances; throughput depends on frame size, bitrate, and error probability/retransmission assumptions.

Retake 2025, Aufgabe 1

Question: State correct properties of optical conductors.

Answer: Optical links support high data rates and long distances and do not need electrical shielding; RJ45 is for twisted-pair copper, not fiber.

Retake 2025, Aufgabe 2

Question: Digital radio with collision probability and modulation/coding.

Answer: Treat independent retries geometrically and combine symbol rate, bits/symbol, and coding overhead for useful data rate.
